# October 5 evaluation timeout recovery

At 13:23 UTC on October 5, both A100 runs were still at their durable step-10,000
checkpoints. The first full validation had repeatedly failed after training
reached that step on October 3. The user services reported 129 U4 / 128 L4
restarts. Neither had completed a full validation or trained past 10k. The
previous October 2 update preceded the failures; the expected evaluation and
training progress did not happen during the intervening roughly 56 hours.

The first recorded failure was October 3 at 05:30 UTC. Rank 1 timed out on an
`ALLGATHER` with `Timeout(ms)=600000`, while rank 5 had not entered that
collective. Round-robin assignment gave rank 5 over twice rank 1's estimated
triangle work. All completed per-protein results existed only in memory, so
each restart repeated the entire validation. GPU utilization and an active
service were therefore insufficient evidence of training progress.

The launch smoke tests covered training, exact data-stream recovery, two
rollouts on the longest protein and a short distributed validation. They did
not exercise the waiting time between ranks for all 97 proteins × 100 rollouts.
The supervisor's three-starts-per-30-minutes rule also allowed this failure
pattern to repeat indefinitely because each attempt lasted longer than ten
minutes.

## Repair

- Stop the two affected services and retain both step-10k checkpoints and their
  complete optimizer, EMA, RNG and data states.
- Give the distributed process group an explicit four-hour collective wait
  budget. PyTorch's [documented NCCL default is ten minutes](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/distributed.html#torch.distributed.init_process_group).
  This changes the communication timeout, not the training schedule or sampler.
- Assign validation proteins greedily by descending length-cubed cost. The
  estimated maximum/minimum load ratio falls from 2.34 to approximately 1.00
  on the frozen 97-protein split. Per-protein rollout seeds, rollout batch size,
  number of rollouts, metric version and candidate pairs remain unchanged.
- Log each rank's validation assignment and per-protein completion so a long
  evaluation can be distinguished from a stalled/restarting process.
- Record the active source hashes and resume point in `runtime.json`, preserving
  the original training provenance. Use a new immutable source snapshot.
- Limit the supervisor to three starts per six hours, so this repeated failure
  pattern stops instead of consuming multiple days of compute.

Regression checks cover exact-once balanced assignment, empty ranks, stable
assignment under input reordering and invariant seeded metrics under
repartitioning. All 84 local tests passed.

## Recovery verification

The new runtime started at 13:30:30 UTC (U4) and 13:30:31 UTC (L4), resuming
the existing step-10k checkpoints. Both full 97 × 100 evaluations completed:
U4 at 13:51:41 UTC and L4 at 13:52:37 UTC. The importer verified all protein
identities, rollout counts and agreement between per-protein means and summaries.

| Arm | Oracle best-of-100 | Frequency consensus |
|---|---:|---:|
| G4-U4 | 0.214326 | 0.211588 |
| G4-L4 | 0.217606 | 0.215978 |

By 14:01 UTC, both models had logged **step 10,050**, confirming 50 optimizer
updates after validation. U4 / L4 losses were 0.126693 / 0.123859, with finite
gradient norms 0.116491 / 0.119648 and 9.783 seconds per step. Both services
remained active with **zero restarts** and no logged post-recovery errors. This
verifies the full evaluation-to-training transition on both production nodes.
The latest durable checkpoints remain at 10k until the scheduled 11k save.

Evidence is recorded in `training-progress-20261005.json`, the refreshed status
snapshots and the recovered fixed-R milestone files.

The model architectures, teacher pool, training configuration, 300k horizon and
validation-only selection policy are unchanged. No held-out data is used.
